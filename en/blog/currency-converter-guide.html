How to Accurately Convert Currencies Before Travel or a Money Transfer

Want to know how much your travel ticket will actually cost in your home currency, or received a payment in a foreign currency and need to calculate its real value? Understanding the basics of currency conversion saves you unpleasant surprises with any international financial transaction.

What is an exchange rate, exactly?

An exchange rate is simply how much of one currency equals one unit of another. When we say "the dollar-to-euro exchange rate is 0.92," it means one dollar equals 0.92 euros at that moment. This number isn't fixed — it constantly changes based on supply and demand forces in the global currency market.

The difference between the bank rate and the real market rate

A point that confuses a lot of people: the rate you see in an online conversion tool (the reference rate in the global market) isn't the same rate you'll actually get when exchanging money at a bank or exchange company. These institutions add a small profit margin (called a spread) on top of the reference rate, so their actual rate is always slightly worse for you — whether buying or selling — than the rate you saw online.

Why do exchange rates change every day?

Exchange rates move constantly during trading days, influenced by many factors: central bank interest rate decisions, a country's economic and political situation, the volume of international trade, and even speculation in financial markets. That's why it's important to check the updated rate at the moment of conversion, not a stale rate from days ago.

Everyday practical uses

  • Trip planning: knowing your approximate budget in the destination country's currency before booking.
  • Shopping abroad: calculating the actual price in your local currency for a product listed in a foreign one.
  • International freelancing: understanding the value of a payment received from a foreign client in a different currency.
  • Tracking investments: monitoring the value of assets or savings held in multiple currencies in one place.

Tips before any real financial transaction

Use the online conversion tool for estimation, general understanding, and advance planning, but for any real transaction (a bank transfer, an actual currency purchase, an international payment), always check the final rate offered by the service provider itself at the time of execution, since that's the rate that will actually apply to your transaction, and it may differ slightly from the reference market rate.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the bank's exchange rate different from the rate I see online?

Banks and exchange companies add a profit margin on top of the real market rate, so the actual rate is always slightly different from the online reference rate.

How often do exchange rates update?

Exchange rates move constantly during trading days, but most free conversion tools update their data at least once a day.

Can I rely on an online conversion tool for an official bank transaction?

Useful for general estimation, but for official transactions, the final rate is always set by the service provider itself at execution time.

Why do some currencies fluctuate more than others?

Currencies of large, stable economies are usually less volatile, while smaller countries' currencies or those under certain conditions can see larger swings.
